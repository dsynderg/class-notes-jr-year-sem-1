## Difficulty progression

### Class 1 — Study Guide 00

1. According to the University’s Statement on Belonging, what is our common primary identity that unites us? — Children of God.
2. What metaphor did Elder Maxwell use to describe the inexhaustible nature of the Book of Mormon and invite us to explore it with more curiosity and attentiveness? — A tourist in the halls of a large mansion.
3. What four things will you account for in Class Preparation reports in Learning Suite? — Pre-class readings, deepening study activity, one salient question, and creative discipleship project time.
4. When are Attendance reports due? — Before class.
5. What Deepening Study Activity will we do for the next couple of weeks? — Journal.
6. When is your Creative Discipleship Project proposal due? — A week from Saturday.
7. What is your favorite line from the Alma versus Korihor rap? — “Do you believe that being duped is the joy that is in their chest?”
8. Fill in the blank from Elder Holland: “Read more _______ and more __________ and with more __________ in mind.” — “Read more slowly and more carefully and with more questions in mind.”

Class 1 personal notes: Brother Taggart loves you so much that he prays for you by name; seek to see people as God does; forgiveness can come through feeling God’s love for that person; creation can bring us closer to God because we are creative beings.

### Class 2 — Study Guide 01

9. How long had it been since Lehi left Jerusalem when the story reaches Alma 30? — About 525 years.
10. What similarities does Mark Wrathall note between Korihor and pre-conversion Alma? — Both are unbelievers, both work against the Church, and Alma led people to sin.
11. What are the four things Korihor says a person cannot know? — You cannot know what you cannot see; you cannot know that there is a Christ; you cannot know the future; and you cannot know that the ancient prophecies are true.
12. Is Korihor perfectly consistent in his arguments? Why or why not? — No. He says absence of evidence is evidence of absence, yet he wants a sign pointing to God; he also says that unless you see God you cannot know God is real.
13. What does Alma tell Korihor that Korihor has no evidence of other than his word? (Alma 30:40) — Korihor has no evidence for his claims other than his own word. Alma points to all things as witnesses and testimonies of God, including the scriptures and the effects seen in the world.
14. What witnesses does Alma point to as testimonies of the reality of God? What did we say in class was his first witness? — The scriptures, the priests, and the planets in their regular motions; the joy in people’s hearts was identified as the first witness.
15. What is the difference between Korihor’s response to being struck dumb and Alma’s response when he was similarly struck dumb years earlier? — Alma chose to repent and felt the need to repent. Korihor knew that only God could have done it but did not feel the need to repent.
16. Who trampled Korihor to death? What does this tell you about their attitude toward beggars and the poor? — The Zoramites. It suggests a harsh attitude toward beggars and the poor.
17. According to Alma 31:5, what had a more powerful effect on people’s minds than the sword? What recent historical example might Alma have been thinking of? — The word of God; Ammon and the word changed the Lamanites more powerfully than battle.
18. What are some important differences between Alma’s prayer and the prayers of the Zoramites from the Rameumptom? — The guide does not record a complete answer. Compare Alma’s humble, pleading, other-focused prayer with the Zoramites’ proud, repetitive, exclusionary prayer.

Class 2 personal notes: Because we cannot observe God directly, we can observe the effects God has on the world. God gives comfort, strength, and joy.

### Class 3 — Study Guide 02

19. How could you explain the presence of the word “synagogue” in the Book of Mormon if the use of Jewish synagogues as currently understood did not develop until after the Babylonian exile? — Two pre-exilic words may translate as synagogue, and translation can be tight or loose; the historical evidence does not require the modern understanding of synagogue to be the only meaning.
20. How often and in how many locations do the poor Zoramites assume they can worship? How do Alma and Amulek respond? — They assume worship can happen once a week and only in the temple they built. Alma and Amulek teach, using Zenos, that people can worship at all times and in all places.
21. Where do the converted Zoramites go after leaving Antionum? How are they received there, and why is this significant? — They are received by the people of Ammon, the Anti-Nephi-Lehies. They are given what they need and find refuge, showing the covenant community receiving and caring for outsiders.
22. What are some differences between knowledge and faith? — Faith is hope in things not seen that are true; knowledge is cognitive experience or something possessed. Hope is more emotional, faith is exercised and volitional, and people act on faith as if it were knowledge.
23. What four things does Alma 32:28 say will happen if the seed planted in our hearts is good? — It will begin to swell; we will say it is a good seed; it enlarges the soul; it enlightens understanding; and it becomes delicious. The notes list these as the four central effects, with the swelling and recognition as the opening signs.
24. What three words are repeated three times in Alma 32:41–43 to describe how we should nourish the growing word-tree? — Diligence, faith, and patience.
25. In Alma 32:3 the poor Zoramites say they were considered dross. What does Amulek say can actually make someone “as dross” in Alma 34? — Forgetting to be charitable.
26. What specific seed, word, or truth do Alma and Amulek invite the impoverished Zoramites to plant in their hearts? — Jesus Christ, described as the tree of life.
27. What is the Church’s official position on whether there is progression between kingdoms? — We do not know; there is no official doctrine settling it.
28. According to Amulek in Alma 34:15–17, what should authentic faith lead to? “Faith unto _____.” — Repentance.

Class 3 personal notes: Humility is a prerequisite to authentic worship; the goal is to embody the goodness of the seed; the end goal is becoming the tree of life yourself.

### Class 4 — Study Guide 03

29. What five items does Alma prepare to entrust to Helaman? — The Nephite records, the brass plates, the Jaredite plates, the interpreters, and the Liahona.
30. In the original context, what is Alma referring to when he says “small and simple things” can bring about God’s great and eternal purposes? — He is specifically referring to the plates or records.
31. What blessings does Alma say the scriptures can bring? (Alma 37:8–10) — They convince people of the truthfulness of Jesus Christ, enlarge hearts, convert thousands of Lamanites, and enlarge the memory of the people.
32. What is equated with wisdom in Alma 37:35? — Keeping the commandments of God.
33. What is a chiasmus? — A Hebrew poetic literary form. The notes describe it imperfectly as a pattern involving repeated or mirrored words and ideas; do not require a rhyme-based definition.
34. What message is at the beginning and ending of Alma’s chiastic message to Helaman? — Keep the commandments and prosper in the land.
35. What does Alma mean, and what does he not mean, when he says that keeping the commandments leads to prosperity? — He does not mean there will be no problems. He means God can give joy and strength and deliver people through trials.
36. What is at the center of Alma’s chiastic counsel to Helaman, and why is it important? — Christ is at the center; Christ is the central focus and the key to the surrounding counsel.
37. Alma previously spoke about tasting light. What did Alma say he tasted during his conversion experience? (Alma 36:24) — Exceeding joy.
38. How old was Alma the elder when his son had the experience with the angel? What might this imply about Alma the younger’s age, and why is it significant? — The guide says Alma the elder was probably 73. This implies Alma the younger may have been an adult or older youth, making the conversion a significant turning point after he had already caused serious harm.

Class 4 personal notes: We believe in grace, but not cheap grace; grace is transformative. A type is a mark or pattern created by something. We should feel animosity toward darkness.

### Class 5 — Study Guide 04

39. What expressions of affection show Alma’s deep love for Shiblon in Alma 38? — Alma says he has great joy in Shiblon, is happy with the way Shiblon lives, and has already had great joy in him.
40. What two-word phrase appears 10 times in Alma 39? — “My son.”
41. Besides sexual sin, what other concerns does Alma bring up with Corianton in Alma 39? — Concerns about the afterlife and the coming of Christ.
42. What is the theological reason behind the affirmation that “God is relationship”? — “Elohim” is plural in the notes, pointing to union between Heavenly Father and Heavenly Mother; divine life is understood relationally.
43. What could it mean to “cross yourself” and go no more after the lusts of your eyes? — Deny or restrain sexual impulses until they can be expressed in the right relationship.
44. What are Elder Holland’s three S-words describing why human sexuality is sacred? — Souls, symbols, and sacraments.
45. What fourth word did we add in lecture, and what did it mean? — Sanctification or exaltation: becoming like Heavenly Father.
46. What sources of hope do Elders Packer and Christofferson offer those for whom the ideal of celestial marriage is not possible? — God is our Father, and the love and generosity manifested in good parents are magnified in God. The sacrifices and successes of people who do not experience the ideal are not diminished.
47. Where does hope lie for Corianton or anyone else who sins? — In Jesus Christ.

### Class 6 — Study Guide 05

48. How would you rewrite Alma 41:10 as a positive statement? — Those who live in the joy of God will be resurrected in the joy of God; righteousness is happiness, and God is God because God is good.
49. What do Terryl and Fiona Givens say about the nature of Heavenly Parents, and what does that teach about Their commandments? — Holiness, joy, and godliness are connected. God is perfectly happy because God embodies a nature fully consistent with happiness; commandments are laws that lead to happiness.
50. What four titles does Alma use to describe God’s plan in Alma 42, and what does this teach about God? — Redemption, happiness, salvation, and mercy. It teaches that God’s plan is oriented toward mercy, restoration, salvation, and happiness.
51. Who punishes us, according to Elder Renlund? — Punishment is a consequence of our own choices, not something God arbitrarily imposes; we are the ones who punish ourselves.
52. Who judges us, according to Alma 41:7? — We are our own judges concerning whether we do good or evil.
53. According to Elder Oaks, what will be more important in the final judgment than what we have done? — The final effects of our acts and thoughts: what we have become.
54. If God ultimately gives us what we want, how does the Fall complicate things? — We live in a fallen world and sometimes choose things we do not truly want; repentance lets us choose again.
55. What is God’s “secret non-weapon” for helping bring about the plan of mercy and allowing agency to be grace-guided? (Alma 42:15) — Jesus Christ and repentance.
56. What does God’s justice mean to Alma? — Despite God’s goodness, He will not force us into goodness.

Class 6 personal notes: Obeying the laws of the universe brings greater happiness; understanding the laws of baking makes a better baker, and understanding coding laws helps someone write better code. The celestial kingdom is a state of being.

### Class 7 — Study Guide 06

57. How old was Moroni when he took command of the Nephite armies? What might be an advantage and disadvantage? (Alma 43:17) — He was 25. Youth could bring energy, courage, and adaptability, but limited experience could be a disadvantage.
58. Who leads almost all of the battles against the Nephites? How does this complicate a “righteous Nephites versus wicked Lamanites” narrative? — Other Nephite leaders lead almost all of the battles. This shows that the conflict cannot be reduced to a simple Nephite-versus-Lamanite moral binary.
59. What possible explanation does Joseph Spencer give for why Helaman does not begin with Alma’s disappearance in Alma 45? — The book’s structure alternates patterns: anti-Christs and war in the first and third quarters, sermons and Ammon’s mission in another section, and Amalickiah’s dissension in the fourth. The placement supports the literary structure.
60. What kind of strife led to the greatest losses of Nephite cities? — Wickedness among the Nephites, civil contention, and internal contention.
61. What is one possible explanation for the identity of the king-men in Alma 51? From which biblical kingly line might they have descended? — They may have been Mulekites, descendants of David.
62. What two things must happen to fulfill what Sharon Harris calls the Book of Mormon covenant, and why is this relevant to the war chapters? — The Lamanite remnant must survive, and the Nephite record must survive. The war chapters concern both peoples and the preservation of the record.
63. What is the difference between “fighting for” and “fighting against”? — Fighting for has the connotation of protecting one’s people; fighting against emphasizes conquering or opposing an enemy.
64. What is the Nephite concept of just war in Alma 48:14–16? What one instance seems to contradict it? — Fight only when necessary, do not give offense, and fight to preserve life. The apparent contradiction is active expansionism in Alma 50:7–11.

### Class 8 — Study Guide 07

65. Of the six epistles Mormon includes in his abridgment, how many are in the war chapters? — Five.
66. What admirable strengths of Moroni are outlined in Alma 48:11–13? — He is strong and mighty, has perfect understanding, does not delight in bloodshed, and delights in the liberty and freedom of his country and his people from bondage and slavery.
67. When Moroni rends his coat and creates the Title of Liberty, what ancient prophecy does he invoke? (Alma 46:19–26) — He shows that the people will be torn if they do not live up to their covenant responsibilities.
68. Is Moroni perfect? What potential shortcoming of Moroni is mentioned eight times in the war chapters? — No. Anger is mentioned.
69. What can we learn from Moroni’s imperfections? — God calls weak vessels, and we too can be used to fulfill God’s work.
70. After saying the powers of hell would have been shaken forever if all people were like Moroni, whom else did Mormon name as “like unto” Moroni? — Helaman, Ammon, and people who act deliberately; the notes also connect Alma and his sons with this comparison.
71. What are some peacemaking phrases from Pahoran’s epistle to Moroni? — “I do not joy in your afflictions”; Pahoran humanizes Moroni; he says Moroni censured him; he rejoices in the greatness of Moroni’s heart; and he proposes working together.
72. When Lamanites no longer want to fight, which group do they join? — The Anti-Nephi-Lehies, or people of Ammon.
73. What do the stripling warriors call Helaman? — Their father.
74. What did the stripling warriors learn from their mothers? — Their mothers had incredible faith in the Lord, which gave the young warriors deep faith.

### Class 9 — Study Guide 08

75. What is happening in the shadows of Nephite society while people focus on political upheavals and glittering progress? — The Gadianton robbers are growing.
76. What word appears 49 times in Helaman 1–13, and what does this suggest Mormon wants us to notice? — “Heart.” It directs attention to the inner condition and desires of the people.
77. What manifestations of the pride cycle appear in Helaman 3:34 and 4:12? — Costly clothing, professing to know the Lord while not truly knowing Him, and related signs of pride and inequality.
78. How can people stop the pride cycle according to Helaman 3:25–36? — Wax strong in humility, pray, acknowledge God, act with sincerity of heart, and cultivate wonder.
79. What percentage of Nephite land did dissenters and Lamanites take in the 58th and 59th years? (Helaman 4:8) — All the lands.
80. What percentage of the land did Moronihah regain in the 60th and 61st years? (Helaman 4:9–10) — Half of their lands back.
81. With society crumbling around him, where does Helaman turn his focus? — His family and children.
82. What does Helaman urge his sons to remember, and what happens because they do? — Remember Jesus Christ. Their remembrance supports faithfulness and spiritual strength.
83. What moment does Kimberly Matheson Berkey call “arguably the most spectacular moment of conversion in the entire Book of Mormon”? — The return of dissenters through the preaching of Lehi and Nephi.

### Class 10 — Study Guide 10

84. What is the name of the Nephite dissenter in prison who once belonged to the Church and urges his friends to repent and cry unto the voice until they have faith? — Abinadab.
85. Kim Matheson calls Helaman 5 “the most hypersaturated intertextual chapter.” What parallels does she see, and what connections exist between the prison miracle and Lehi’s tree-of-life vision? — Shaking prisons, luminous prophets, abandoning judgment seats, miraculous fire, and angelic ministrations; these connect the prison experience with major Book of Mormon revelation and conversion scenes, including Lehi’s vision.
86. What were results of the mass Lamanite conversion in Helaman 5:49–52 and 6:1, 6–8? — Everything changes: there is much peace, the Lamanites give lands back, and the Lamanites begin to preach.
87. How long did Nephi preach without success among the Nephites? (Helaman 6:6 and 7:1–3) — Six years; they rejected all his words.
88. What does Elder Wirthlin say materialism can lead to? — Covetousness can become an obsession that consumes thoughts, drains resources, and leads to unhappiness.
89. What results from Nephi’s miraculous declaration of the murder of the chief judge and the identity of the murderer? — People believe the rest of Nephi’s words, especially his message about the coming Messiah.
90. What does God tell Nephi when Nephi is discouraged about his apparent lack of success? (Helaman 10:4–5) — Nephi is blessed because he has not feared the people and has kept God’s commandments; God will bless him forever and do what Nephi asks.
91. Why did God give Nephi the sealing power? (Helaman 10:5, 7) — Because Nephi was faithful, did not seek his own life, and kept God’s commandments; the power was given so his actions could serve God’s purposes.
92. What similarities exist between Nephi and the Old Testament prophet Elijah? — Both are miracle workers with a flair for the dramatic, both suffer discouragement, and both denounce murder.
93. How does Nephi use the sealing power, and what is the result? — He seals the skies and causes a drought.
94. What seems to be Mormon’s point in his hymn about the earth in Helaman 12, especially verses 7–17 and 23? — Humans do not move when God speaks; we should become more like humble dirt and acknowledge God.

Class 10 personal notes: Cry unto the Lord until you have faith. Focusing on objects can lead us to objectify people. If Nephi’s prophecies are fulfilled, the rest of his message is also supported.

### Class 11 — Study Guide 11

95. Why does Samuel initially say he was rejected by the Nephites? (Helaman 14:10) — Because he was a Lamanite.
96. How would you describe the tone of Samuel’s preaching? — Threatening, urgent, violent, and angry in its surface tone.
97. How does Elder Christofferson suggest interpreting “wrath” in scripture? — As consequential reality arising from widespread disobedience, rather than as arbitrary divine irritability.
98. Is it better for mental and physical health to believe in a punitive God or a loving God? — A loving God.
99. Which word or derivatives appear more in Samuel’s teachings than in any other three consecutive chapters in the Book of Mormon? — “Repent.”
100. What phrases from current Church leaders help explain the positive nature of repentance? — God lovingly chastens and invites us to change our minds, knowledge, and lives; without repentance there is no real progress.
101. What does Samuel say will happen to Nephite riches if they do not repent? — They will become slippery.
102. What does the arc of Samuel’s message teach about God or the ultimate tone of his preaching? — Beneath the warning, the message is ultimately about the hope of Christ.
103. How could you apply the fact that no arrows or stones could hit Samuel to your own life? — The Lord may protect us in ways that cannot be explained, though do not assume every difficult outcome will be prevented.

Class 11 personal notes: Be open to learning from nonmembers. Justice can make people angry; change your life because God loves you, and let justice become a catalyst.

### Class 12 — Study Guide 12

104. How does David Bokovoy explain “hate” in an ancient Near Eastern context, and how does that relate to Samuel saying God hated the Lamanites because their deeds were evil? — “Hate” may refer to lacking covenantal love or covenant loyalty. The notes say many Lamanites lacked that covenantal love.
105. What are possible explanations for 3 Nephi 2:15 saying the Lamanites’ skin became white like the Nephites? — The notes say it is unlikely to concern literal skin pigmentation and more likely describes crossing an insider/outsider boundary.
106. What does President Oaks urge Church members to do regarding racism? — Do not practice racism; unite to root it out.
107. How did unbelievers describe the believers’ joy and faith? (3 Nephi 1:6) — Their joy and hope were vain.
108. What are Christ’s first nine words to worried Nephi in 3 Nephi 1:13? — “Lift up your head and be of good cheer.”
109. What lessons come from the fulfillment of Samuel’s seemingly impossible prophecies and the statement that everything came to pass according to the prophets? — Trust that God fulfills His word and watch steadfastly for the appointed day.
110. Did everyone believe when the signs were fulfilled? (3 Nephi 1:22) — No. Most of the people believed, but not everyone.
111. How did the Nephites protect themselves against the Gadianton robbers? What might it look like to be “in one body” today? (3 Nephi 4:3–4) — They united with the Lamanites and prepared to defend themselves. Today it could mean cooperating and standing together for protection and shared covenant purposes.

113. What does Mormon prophesy will surely happen because God has covenanted to do it? (3 Nephi 5:23–26) — No answer was recorded in the guide. Treat this as a review item and use the course text to supply the exact answer.

### Class 13 — Study Guide 13

114. What passage did the woman on the instructor’s mission show to justify saying her God was not as angry as the instructor’s? — 3 Nephi 9:2–12.
115. What phrase appears five times in 3 Nephi 9:5–11 describing why people were destroyed before the Savior’s visit? — “I did this that the blood of the prophets shall not come unto me against them.”
116. According to 3 Nephi 6:12, what two things determined the ranks or classes that divided the people? — Education and wealth; pride is also noted as connected to the division.
117. What was nearly completely “broken up” in 3 Nephi 6:14? — The Church.
118. What three groups became angry with and ultimately killed the men inspired from heaven or prophets in 3 Nephi 6:20–25? — Judges, high priests, and lawyers.
119. How often did Nephi 3 receive visits from angels? — Daily.
120. What type of natural disaster do some scholars, beginning with Bart Kowallis, propose as the source of the thunderings, earthquakes, and thick darkness accompanying the Savior’s death? — A volcanic eruption or volcano.
121. What is the ultimate arc of the voice’s message in the darkness? — The love of God: “If ye will come unto me ye shall have eternal life.”
122. What lines from the second half of 2 Nephi 9 and from 2 Nephi 10 teach about the true nature of God? — The notes emphasize that He will protect us.
123. What did the instructor learn about God from burning a couch? — God is conscious of or mindful of you.
124. Fill in the blank from Elder Holland: “God does not now nor will He ever do to you a ___________, _________, ______ thing—ever.” — “Destructive, malicious, unfair.”
125. What maternal image does Jesus use in 3 Nephi 10:3–6 to show His care and desire to bless and protect? — A hen gathering her chickens under her wings: “How oft would I gather you, but ye would not.”
